Lofty Hub — design check

Twenty-three findings against Vibe 4, this project's design system and WCAG 2.2 AA. Every finding names its evidence — a hex value, a measured ratio, a string on screen — and the ones with a drawn fix link to the mockup that shows it.

Read: DESIGN.md, PRODUCT.md, CLAUDE.md, app/src/theme/*, SidePanel.tsx, SortableTable.tsx, PanelExpand.tsx, app/package.json (@vibe/core 4.5.30), and the prototype captures in docs/comparison-screenshots/.

The captures are of the prototype, not the React app. Where a finding is only provable from a capture it says so, and it should be re-checked against the running app before anyone spends a day on it.

Re-checked against the design system update

The design system moved after round one: a single neutral family, a single-accent data-viz ramp, and a data-viz component set. The mockups have been brought onto it. Nothing in the findings below changed as a result.

APPLIED
Flint replaces the cool greys

Mid Grey #d1d3d4 is no longer a UI border, and #e1e1d9 and #f9f9f6 are retired. 116 literals across the five screens now read Flint 100 for the page, 200 for rules and tracks, 300 for card and table borders.

APPLIED
Charts are single-accent

Orange and Eco Green may no longer appear in the same view, so the board's four-step green phase ramp is re-cut on the orange ladder — Orange 40, Orange 70, Crisp Orange, Orange deep — and the green counter tints become Flint 200. Reports was already one orange series on a neutral track.

OPEN
The orange split still stands

The update keeps white ink on filled orange and adds the rule that orange fills are for 16px semibold and up. Buttons here carry 14px labels, so the mockups keep #c2543c — the system's own --lofty-orange-pressed, named for exactly this case — for anything that carries text. See P0-2.

AVAILABLE, NOT YET USED
Data viz and dark mode

StatTile, BarChart, DonutStat, MeterRow, ChartLegend and StatusChip now ship in the system; the Reports panels in 4a are still hand-built and would drop onto them. tokens/dark.css also ships — the mockups are light-only and no dark screen has been drawn.

P0 — blocks a task or fails AA

P0-1

Dates render in US order on an Adelaide product

Evidence. The job drawer's selections tracker and the new-job dialog both show the empty date placeholder as mm/dd/yyyy, and a filled row reads 07/13/2026 — a date that only parses one way. PRODUCT.md states dates are Adelaide calendar days. A native input type="date" takes its order from the browser locale, so the same record reads differently to two people in the same office.

Fix. The design system's DatePicker, formatting dd/mm/yyyy everywhere, and one date formatter in the repository seam rather than per screen. Nine rows of it drawn in 1a.

P0-2

Orange as the primary accent needs its darker step, or every filled button fails

Evidence. You chose the design system's hierarchy — orange leads. But the system's --text-color-on-primary is white on Crisp Orange #f47e63, which measures 2.62:1. Foundation Black on the same orange is 3.91:1 — enough for a boundary, not enough for a 14px label. The repo reached the same number independently and wrote it into DESIGN.md: "2.6:1 on white… not used for meaning".

Fix, and it invents no colour. --lofty-orange-pressed #c2543c already exists in the system and clears 4.5:1 with white. Make it the orange that carries text or meaning — filled buttons, links, focus text — and keep Crisp Orange for what it is good at: decorative fills, the 3px active-tab underline, large display figures, the first data series. That reconciles the system's orange-first hierarchy with the repo's contrast contract without a fourth place for colour to live. Every mockup uses this split.

P0-3

The three fields people change most are native selects

Evidence. In the job drawer capture, Who it's with, Team, Phase and Build stage are all browser select elements — grey chevron, OS menu, no search. DESIGN.md lists this under Known limitations for the prototype and says the React app uses the real component; the capture is what a reviewer sees, so it is worth confirming which is deployed. Reassigning a job across eight stages and forty people is the drawer's most-used action, and a native select gives no type-ahead beyond first letter and no way to show the stage colour.

Fix. Vibe 4 Dropdown with search on anything over eight options, and the same control in the drawer, the dialog and the toolbar. Drawn in 1a and 2a.

P0-4

The board breaks its own colour rule, and its own product rule

Evidence. DESIGN.md: "Colour on containers encodes phase. Colour on records encodes health. Never both on one element." PRODUCT.md: "Status pills are off on cards until there is a plan for calculated health." In the board capture the columns carry a coloured strip and every card carries a filled On track / At risk / Stalled / Council hold pill, in green, amber and grey — four health values with no stated calculation behind them, sitting on top of a phase ramp.

Why it is a P0 and not a preference. Two colour systems on one card means a scanning eye cannot tell which one it is reading, and the pill is the louder of the two — so the board reads as a health board when health is the value nobody has defined. This is the same failure as the "45% on track" figure the repo already deleted, in colour instead of numbers.

Fix. Pick one. Either health comes back with a stated calculation and the phase ramp goes quiet, or the pills come off until it does. Both drawn side by side in 3a.

P0-5

The floating + covers the drawer's own actions again

Evidence. A round orange + sits at the bottom-right of the viewport, and the drawer occupies the right edge — so the + lands on top of the drawer's content and, on a phone where the drawer is full width, on top of where its save row sits. SidePanel.tsx carries the note that this exact collision already happened once: "the ai button at the bottom of the screen covers the save button". The fix then was to give side work one shell; a second floating layer puts the bug back.

Fix. The create action belongs in the page's title row where the other primaries are, and the board's last row is the second way in that Lofty asked for. No floating layer over the drawer. 2b shows the inline row; 3a shows the toolbar placement.

P0-6

Checkbox rows in the drawer are below the target floor at panel width

Evidence. The selections tracker puts a 16px checkbox, a label and a date field on one row inside a panel whose default width is min(460px, 100vw). PRODUCT.md commits to 40px minimum targets in the drawer and calls the drawer at panel width the phone layout. The rows in the capture are visibly tighter than 40px, and this is the surface a PM uses one-handed on a ladder — the exact scenario in the brief.

Fix. 48px rows, the whole row as the checkbox's hit area, the date as a second line rather than a second column. 1c is that at 390px.

P1 — the consistency findings

These are the nine you selected on the form, with what was actually observed in each. The systemic fix for all nine is the pattern register at the foot of this page: one canonical answer per column, written down, so the next screen inherits it instead of choosing again.

P1-1

Toolbars: labels beside controls, and the primary action inside the filter row

The jobs toolbar reads View [select] Group [select] Date [field] Filter by [+ Add filter] [Clear] [+ New job] — six labelled controls and the page's primary action on one line, with the row count floated to the far right. A label beside every control doubles the width of the row and pushes the filters off-screen at tablet size; the primary action sitting among the filters means the loudest thing on the row is not the thing the row is for. One canonical toolbar, on every list screen, in 3a and reused unchanged in 3b and 4a.

P1-2

The primary action is in three different places

Board: + New job, inline in the filter row. New-job dialog: Create 1201-05, bottom-right of a footer. Job drawer: Open job file, a filled green button a third of the way down the panel body, beside an outlined Request changes. Three placements is three things to learn. Canonical: title row, right-aligned, one per region — dialogs keep the footer because a footer is a title row at the other end of a form.

P1-3

Button labels: three grammars and a typed plus sign

Observed on two screens: + New job, + Add filter, Clear, Ask, Open job file, Request changes, Create 1201-05, Cancel. The + is a character inside the label rather than the icon set's Add glyph, so it does not inherit icon colour or size; Clear and Ask are verbs with no object; Create 1201-05 names a number nobody has agreed to yet. Canonical: verb + object, sentence case, the icon set's glyph in leftIcon — New job, Add filter, Clear filters, Ask about this job, Create job.

P1-4

Row click: the drawer is the rule and the exceptions are not marked

The must-have is written and the shell exists — SidePanel.tsx, expand, resize, Escape, selection in the URL. What is missing is a way to tell, at a glance, which rows honour it. In the captures a board card, a table row and a project card are three different shapes with three different hover treatments, and a card that opens a panel looks identical to one that navigates. Canonical: any record row or card gets the same hover wash rgba(65,64,66,.08), the same --box-shadow-xs lift, and opens the panel. Anything that navigates instead is a link and looks like one.

P1-5

Tables: no shared component, so density and headers drift

SortableTable.tsx says it plainly: "Not a table component… The tables in this app each render their own cells and there is no reason for them to stop." That was the right call for sorting and it is the wrong call for everything else — row height, header treatment, the sort arrow, the selected row, the empty row and the sticky header are now decided per page. The arrow is also a typed ↑/↓ character rather than the icon set's chevron, and it shows an inactive up-arrow on every unsorted column, which reads as "sorted ascending" at a glance. Canonical density and header in 5b.

P1-6

Create: a dialog, a side panel and an inline row all exist, with no rule for which

New job is a centred dialog. SidePanel.tsx was built to replace exactly that dialog, and its own comment argues the panel is better because "the list stays visible and usable behind it". InlineNewProjectRow.tsx is a third way. All three are defensible; having all three without a rule is not. Canonical, and it matches Lofty's "both, they can add either way": inline row for a record that is just a name in a list, side panel for a record with fields, centred dialog only for a decision — move, clone, delete. That retires the new-job dialog. 2a is it as a panel, 2b as the inline row.

P1-7

Empty states: the three kinds are not distinguished

Nothing in the captures shows a column with no cards, a filter that matched nothing, or a report with no bound data — and the repo's strongest principle ("never fill a gap with a plausible value") makes empty the most common state until the import runs, not the rarest. Three different states get three different answers: first use names the action that fills it, no-results names the filter to loosen and offers to clear it, and an unbound value renders its {{ tokColumn }} token. All three drawn: first-use in 3a, no-results in 5b, unbound in 4a.

P1-8

Bulk actions and selection do not exist yet

No row checkboxes, no selection count, no bulk bar in any capture. The board has drag-to-move for one card at a time, and the import is about to land 801 jobs that will need re-teaming in batches. Canonical: a leading checkbox column on every table, and one bulk bar that replaces the filter row when the selection is non-empty — count, two or three actions, Undo in the toast. Shown in 5b.

P1-9

Saved views, tabs and the view switcher are three ways to say the same thing

The board carries a View select (Board / Table / Calendar / Gantt), a Group select, a breadcrumb reading Jobs › Board › Grouped by Stage, an h2 reading Board — by Stage, and SavedViewTabs.tsx for saved views. Four of those describe the same state, one of them twice. Canonical: view is a Tabs row (the system's rule — tabs switch between views of the same object), grouping is a control in the filter row, saved views are chips above the tabs, and the breadcrumb states location only. Drawn in 3b.

P1-10

Drag-to-order has no keyboard twin on screen

PRODUCT.md makes up/down buttons on every draggable row non-optional, and the board's own hint reads "drag cards between columns to move a job" with no stated alternative. Board cards carry no move control, so stage changes are drag-only there while Settings → Processes has the buttons. Canonical: every ordered or movable thing has a visible non-drag path — up/down on rows, a Move menu item on cards. 5a shows the row; the same menu is on the cards in 3a.

P1-11

Job numbers appear in two formats

PRODUCT.md specifies a three-digit sequence — 1042-001. Every number in the captures is two digits — 1209-02, 1201-05, 1210-01. The number is the thing people say out loud and write on contracts, and it is about to be the join key for 801 imported rows. Settle the format before the import, not after.

P1-12

Active nav reads as a button; a state badge reads as an action

The current page is marked with a white filled box on the dark header, which is the shape of a button — so the loudest element in the top bar is the page you are already on. Two items to its right, Dummy data is a filled orange pill in the same row: a state badge wearing a primary action's clothes, next to a search field. This is the prototype's header. The React app has already moved navigation to a left rail (AppShell.tsx: eight destinations, Admin behind a cog in the header), which resolves the first half. Canonical for the rail: current location is the #fae4d5 tinted row with Foundation Black text; environment state is an outlined Label, not a fill, and sits with the account menu rather than in the nav run.

P1-13

Coloured left-border strips are doing work the system gave to nothing

The drawer's "Ask about this job" block is a tinted panel with a coloured left-edge strip, and the at-risk note on a board card is another. The design system is explicit — "Coloured left-border accent strips are not part of this system" — and the reason is that the strip is the one device that cannot carry a word, so it means whatever the reader guesses. Canonical: AttentionBox for anything advisory (icon + word + full border), and Ask moves to a tertiary button in the panel header, where it stops competing with the record.

P1-14

A disabled primary with no reason given

In the new-job dialog, Create 1201-05 is dimmed while the form is incomplete and nothing says which field is holding it. The rule is to explain why something is disabled or not to disable it: keep the button live, validate on blur, and on submit move focus to the first field that needs an answer. 2a shows the live button with the field-level error.

P2 — polish, and two deliberate trade-offs

  • The board's coach-mark tooltip ("See everything in Sales & acquisition") renders as an opaque block over the column header it is describing. Anchor it below its trigger with an offset, or make it a Tipseen with a dismiss.
  • Two dashes are doing three jobs: Board — by Stage (em dash), Demo Admin — … (truncation), — not set — (empty value). Use the icon set's chevron for hierarchy, an ellipsis for truncation, and leave empty values genuinely empty.
  • The Type row on every board card shows "Residential" for every card in the capture. A column whose value never varies is a column, not a card field — move it to the filter row.
  • Trade-off, stated. The general UI floor asks for 16px body text. Vibe's ramp and DESIGN.md put table and card body at 14/20 with 12/16 secondary, and that is what the mockups use — the product's own written rule wins. The cost is real on a phone in daylight, so the mockups raise the drawer's primary facts to 16/24 and keep 12px for labels only, never for a value.
  • Trade-off, stated. The system reserves the full pill for Avatar and Counter. The status chips in the mockups are 4px radius for that reason, which will look tighter than the captures.

What needs your decision

  1. Health on cards: back with a calculation, or off? P0-4 cannot be fixed without this answer, and it changes the board.
  2. Job number: two digits or three? Before the 801-row import.
  3. Does the new-job dialog get retired for the side panel? The panel's own code argues it should have been already.
  4. Is the deployed app still using native selects and date inputs, or is that only true of the prototype these captures came from? It changes whether P0-1 and P0-3 are hours or days.
